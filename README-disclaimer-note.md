# On the disclaimer

Short answer: **you do not need a new one.**

Your existing `/disclaimer` already carries a fire-specific clause, and it is
well drafted:

> Where we discuss foam, fillings, fire grades (such as CMHR or Crib 5) and fire
> regulations, this is general guidance, not legal or compliance advice.
> Fire-safety law \u2014 including the UK Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety)
> Regulations \u2014 is detailed and changes over time... Always confirm the current
> regulations that apply to your work, and seek professional or legal advice for
> commercial projects.

On top of that, every country page now carries its own prominent warning saying
it is written by a British upholsterer, that it is informational rather than
legal advice, listing its sources, and printing the date it was last checked.

That is proportionate. More legal boilerplate does not make wrong information
safe \u2014 accuracy and sourcing do, and the pages have both.

## Two small changes made

**Every fire page now links to `/disclaimer`.** It was the one obvious gap:
pages carrying the most legally sensitive material on the site were not pointing
at the page written for exactly that purpose.

## One edit worth making by hand

Your disclaimer's fire clause names only the UK regulations. The site now covers
five jurisdictions. One sentence fixes it \u2014 add after the existing fire
paragraph:

> Where we describe fire regulations in other countries, we do so as working
> upholsterers rather than as qualified advisers in those jurisdictions. Those
> pages list their sources and the date they were last checked. Verify the
> current position with the relevant national authority before relying on it.

It is a two-minute edit to `disclaimer.html` and I have not automated it, because
the page is hand-written prose and a script would make a mess of the surrounding
text.
