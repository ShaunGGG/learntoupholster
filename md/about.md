# About Learn to Upholster

*Learn to Upholster*

> Learn to Upholster is a free upholstery reference and companion to the book The Working Upholsterer's Bible, by master upholsterer Shaun Greenwood.

Learn to Upholster is a free, in-depth upholstery reference, written from real workshop experience and shared so that anyone can learn the craft.

## Who’s behind it

The site is written and run by Shaun Greenwood, a master upholsterer with thirty-four years at the bench and AMUSF accreditation. He runs [Greenwood Upholstery](https://www.greenwoodupholstery.com/) in Hebden Bridge, West Yorkshire, with his wife Pat, and is the author of [The Working Upholsterer’s Bible](https://www.learntoupholster.com/buy-the-book).

### Starting at the bottom

I started at sixteen at Vale Bridgecraft, on modern and traditional upholstery. You don’t begin with a chair. You begin with valances, the skirt that runs round the bottom of a sofa, and you do that until it comes out right every time. From there it was finishing off outarms and outbacks, then cutting, then frame making, and only after all of that, upholstery itself.

That order matters, and it is the reason this site is built the way it is. Learning the trade the other way round, cover first and frame last, is how people end up able to copy a job but not diagnose one. I spent a good twenty years at Vale, and by the end I had worked every stage of a piece from bare timber to finished cover. It is also why the book starts with [the anatomy of an upholstered piece](https://www.learntoupholster.com/the-anatomy-of-an-upholstered-piece) rather than with technique.

### Vehicle trimming and fabrication

I moved on to vehicle trimming and fabrication at Mellor Coachcraft, building internal components for local authority vehicles. I trimmed and fabricated my own parts and ran the sub-assembly department, hands on rather than from an office.

Vehicle work is a different discipline from furniture. Tolerances are tighter, materials are specified rather than chosen, and everything has to pass inspection and be documented as having passed. That is where I learned to work to a standard somebody else sets and to prove it afterwards, which is why the [fire regulations](https://www.learntoupholster.com/fire-regulations) sections here go into the detail they do instead of waving at them.

### Self-employed, then Greenwood Upholstery

After Mellor I worked self-employed, restoring furniture for a range of companies. That period covered the widest spread of work I have done: antiques, period pieces, one-offs, and a great many things nobody had touched in fifty years.

Greenwood Upholstery is the workshop my wife Pat and I run together at Cragg Vale, near Hebden Bridge. It is a family business. We cover traditional and modern re-upholstery, antique restoration, caravans and leisure vehicles, and hold approved contractor status with Haven and Bourne. Everything on this site is written from work that came through that workshop, which is also why there is a page on [setting one up](https://www.learntoupholster.com/the-workshop) and a whole [section on the business side](https://www.learntoupholster.com/business) that most upholstery books leave out.

## Teaching

I have taught throughout my career: apprentices, colleagues, and people coming into the trade later in life. Not one narrow speciality but the whole spectrum, upholstery, frame making, cutting and [sewing](https://www.learntoupholster.com/sewing).

Traditional skills are in demand and the people who hold them are getting older. Every technique that does not get passed on becomes a little harder for the next person to find, and some of them will not be written down anywhere else. That is the reason this site exists in the form it does, and the reason none of it sits behind a paywall. If you are weighing up whether this is a trade worth entering, start with [whether you need formal training](https://www.learntoupholster.com/blog/do-you-need-training-to-be-an-upholsterer) and the [State of the Trade survey](https://www.learntoupholster.com/state-of-the-trade).

## Building tools for the trade

I have taken to AI rather than avoided it, but only where it solves a problem I actually have in the workshop. The oldest one in the trade is that customers cannot picture it. You hand somebody a swatch the size of a postcard and ask them to imagine a whole three-piece suite in it, and quite reasonably they hesitate, sometimes for weeks.

So I built [Visualiser Pro](https://pro.learntoupholster.com/), which lets a workshop show a customer their own chair in the fabric they are considering. There is a free version on my fabric shop, [Bodella](https://bodella.co.uk/), for choosing from the range there, and on the Greenwood site a customer can submit a photo for a quote and then see their own furniture in different covers. That last one has gone down particularly well. It turns a conversation that used to stall into one that finishes, and a customer who can see it is a customer who decides.

The [free visualiser](https://www.learntoupholster.com/fabric-visualiser), the [cost calculator](https://www.learntoupholster.com/reupholstery-cost-calculator) and the [yardage calculator](https://www.learntoupholster.com/fabric-yardage) on this site all came out of the same instinct: build the thing you keep wishing existed, then leave it where anyone can use it.

## What this site is

Learn to Upholster is the free companion to the book The Working Upholsterer’s Bible. It’s a growing reference covering both traditional and modern upholstery: stripping and frame repair, webbing and springing, stuffing and stitched edges, foam construction, top covers, buttoning, trimming and loose covers, alongside the history of the craft and a working glossary. There are also free, practical tools — a fabric calculator, a reupholstery cost estimator, a deep-buttoning calculator and a foam selector — the kind of sums an upholsterer does at the bench.

## Why it’s free

Upholstery is a craft that’s easy to lose and hard to learn from scratch. Good information is scattered, and much of the real know-how stays inside workshops. This site gathers as much of it as possible in one place, for nothing, so that anyone — a beginner re-covering their first dining chair, or a working upholsterer checking a detail — can use it. The adverts and occasional affiliate links help cover the cost of running it.

## How the content is made

The chapters and tools are written from hands-on experience in a live workshop, then illustrated and tested. Where we recommend a tool or material, it’s something we would actually use. We aim to be accurate and practical; if you spot something that could be better, we’d like to hear about it.

## The book

The Working Upholsterer’s Bible is a complete, illustrated course covering the same ground in depth, from first principles to finished projects. This site mirrors and extends it.

The Association of Master Upholsterers & Soft Furnishers featured the free toolbox and the book in its July/August 2026 member Spotlight.
