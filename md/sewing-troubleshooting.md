# When the machine misbehaves

*Sewing · Troubleshooting*

> Upholstery sewing machine troubleshooting: skipped stitches, thread breaking, puckering, tension and feed problems, in the order worth checking.

Canonical: https://www.learntoupholster.com/sewing-troubleshooting

Something has gone wrong and there is a job waiting. Find the symptom, work down the list in order — the cheap checks are first for a reason.

Before anything else: change the needle. It is the cheapest thing in the workshop and the most frequent culprit. A needle bends long before it looks bent, and a bend of a fraction of a millimetre moves it out of the hook’s path. Machine shops report a great many machines brought in for skipped stitches that sew perfectly the moment a new needle goes in.

Then remember the order: threading, needle, tension. Roughly four times out of five the fault is one of those three and not the machine.

## Skipped stitches

For a stitch to form, the hook has to pass through the loop of thread that appears at the needle eye as the needle rises a few millimetres off its lowest point. Miss that loop and you get a gap. Everything below is a reason the hook and the loop failed to meet.

- Change the needle. First, always.
- Check the needle is in the right way round and fully home
- Check the needle size against the thread
- Rethread the machine completelyup, so the tension discs are open. Threading with the foot down leaves the thread sitting outside the discs and no amount of adjustment will fix it.
- Check the material is being held down
- Check the throat plate
- Only then suspect timing
## Thread breaking or shredding

Usually friction somewhere in the thread path, or a mismatch between thread and needle. The thread is telling you where it is being chafed.

- Needle too fine for the thread
- Burr on the needle, hook, throat plate or thread guides
- Top tension too tight
- Wrong twistZ twist. S twist untwists as it runs through the machine and frays for no other reason. See the [thread guide](https://www.learntoupholster.com/sewing-thread).
- Unbonded thread
- Thread coming off the cone badly
## A nest of thread under the fabric at the start of a seam

Almost never a tension fault, though it looks like one.

- Hold both thread tails behind the foot as you start
- Start a few millimetres in, then reverse back
- Check the upper thread is actually in the tension discs
## Puckered seams

The two layers are arriving at the needle at different rates, or the thread is pulling the seam up after it is sewn.

- Top tension too tight
- Drop feed on a job that needs compound feed[machines](https://www.learntoupholster.com/sewing-machines).
- Blunt needle
- Stitch too short for the cloth
## Uneven or loose stitches on one side

Loops on the underside mean the top tension is losing; loops on top mean the bobbin is. The loose side is the side that is failing.

- Rethread the top with the presser foot up
- Check the bobbin case for lint
- Check the bobbin is wound evenly
- Adjust the top tension, not the bobbin
## Material not feeding evenly

Something is holding one surface back, or the feed is not gripping it.

- Vinyl or coated cloth sticking to the foot
- Feed dogs clogged or worn
- Presser foot pressure wrong
- Wrong feed type for the work
## When it is worth stopping

Timing, hook clearance and a bent needle bar are real faults and they do happen. But they are the last things to suspect, not the first, and they need tools and someone who knows the machine. If you have worked through the list for a symptom and nothing has changed, that is the point to stop guessing and get it looked at — rather than three hours in, having moved every adjustment on the machine and lost your starting point.

Write down what you change. The single most useful habit in machine troubleshooting. Move one thing at a time, note it, and test. Half the machines that end up genuinely out of adjustment got there during an attempt to fix something else.
