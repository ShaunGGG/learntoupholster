## What a quote actually costs you

A quote feels like admin. It is not. It is unpaid labour, and it is worth measuring.

A home visit to look at a three-piece suite is an hour there and back if you are lucky,
half an hour on site, and half an hour afterwards working out the numbers and writing it
up. Two hours, plus fuel and van wear. At any sensible shop rate that is a meaningful sum,
and you might do six of them to win two jobs.

Give all of that away and the customers who do buy are subsidising the ones who never
intended to.

## The arrangement that works

Most workshops settle on something like this.

- **Photographs: free.** Quick, no travel, and it filters out the people who were never serious. Ask for the whole piece, both arms, the back, any damage, and a close-up of the existing cover.
- **Home visit: charged.** A stated fee, agreed before you set off.
- **Credited if you win the work.** The fee comes off the final invoice, so a genuine customer pays nothing extra and a tyre-kicker pays for your time.

That last point is what makes it palatable. You are not charging people to quote. You are
charging people who do not go ahead.

## How to say it

Do not be apologetic about it, and do not over-explain.

> "I can give you a quote from photographs free of charge — if you send me a few shots I'll
> come back with a figure. If you'd rather I came out and looked at it properly, that's £X,
> which comes off the job if you go ahead."

Nobody who intends to have the work done objects to that. The ones who object have told you
something useful about themselves.

## When to visit anyway

There are jobs where you should get in the van regardless.

- Anything large, unusual, or built into a room
- Contract and commercial work, where the relationship is worth more than one job
- Antique or high-value pieces where a mistake is expensive
- A customer who has been recommended by someone you already work with

Judgement, not policy. The point of charging is not to collect fees, it is to stop giving
away unlimited time to people who are shopping around.

## Estimate or quote?

Two different things, and confusing them causes arguments.

An **estimate** is an informed guess and can change. A **quote** is a fixed price you are
committing to. Say which one you are giving, every time, and if it is a quote make clear
what it covers — see [the chair being worse underneath than expected](/business/worse-underneath-than-expected)
for how to handle what you could not see.
